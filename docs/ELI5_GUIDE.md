# SynSync Pro in plain English

**Audience:** someone who has never used synthesized audio or encountered the term “brainwave entrainment.”

## The 60-second explanation

Think of music as a recorded road trip. SynSync is more like a **programmable route planner for sound**. Instead of pressing Play on one fixed recording, you pick a protocol—a recipe telling the browser which tones to make, how they should change over time and when each segment ends.

A **carrier** is the pitch you hear. A **beat frequency** is a slower rhythmic difference or pulse. **Binaural** presentation uses two slightly different pitches, one for each ear. **Isochronic** presentation uses a distinctly pulsing sound. A **phase** is one scheduled part of a longer listening session.

A recipe can target an experience such as winding down or focusing. It cannot promise what your brain will do, and the app does not diagnose or measure a therapeutic outcome simply because a recipe played.

## What you control

You select a protocol, read its cautions, decide whether to proceed, set the volume, and start/stop playback. Some visualizers change what you see, not the facts about what your body is doing. Advanced controls may change how sound is presented; they do not make the software a medical device.

## A first-time journey

Choose a gentle listed protocol in Guided mode. Look at the description and time required. Start with low system volume; complete the safety screen; press Play. Notice whether you are comfortable. Stop when you want. You can choose another session later, but **you do not owe the app your personal information or continued listening**.

## Three common misunderstandings

- **“A study exists, so this particular protocol is proven.”** No. Published research on a frequency, EEG method or sound modality does not automatically validate this exact signal, implementation or promised outcome.
- **“The pretty visual proves my brain is synchronized.”** No. It is a visualization of app or sound data, not automatically a biological measurement.
- **“A downloadable app and a GitHub repository mean a finished clinical release.”** No. This repository is a pre-release candidate with pending license/signing decisions.
